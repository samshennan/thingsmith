# Native-speaker check — send-ready form

Hand this to the principal with the shortlist filled in. It is the step a machine screen
cannot do: catching current slang, regional readings, and what a word *feels* like to
someone who lives in the language.

**Standard:** three native speakers per language, **living in that country now** (Catchword's
rule — expats lose slang within a few years). Not colleagues who have seen the brief. Not
the founder's family. Paid is fine; Prolific, Upwork or a local translator network all work.

**How to send it:** one person at a time, names in a different random order for each person,
and **never explain the names or the product first.** The first reaction is the data.

---

## Copy below this line

need. There are no right answers, and "this is bad" is very useful.

**Your language:** ________  **Country you live in:** ________  **City/region:** ________

## Reading the answers (for whoever runs the project)

| Signal | Rule |
|---|---|
| Any respondent reports a crude, insulting, medical or politically loaded meaning | **Kill.** One credible report is enough; do not average it away |
| Two of three write a different pronunciation from the target | **Kill** for that market (the four-language veto) |
| Spelling-back: fewer than 2 of 3 correct | Fail — same rule as the five-person test |
| Three-word associations | Compare against the brief's job sentence. Score Positioning on the card from these, not from the team's hopes |
| Trust score | Directional only at n=3. Do not report an average as a finding |

Write every kill into the KILLED list with the respondent's own words.
