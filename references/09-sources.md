# Sources and method notes

Thingsmith combines original workflow instructions and scripts with ideas from published naming practice. These references are background reading, not endorsements or proof of equivalent outcomes. The creative scorecard is Thingsmith's eight-dimension /80 comparison; verification evidence remains separate.

## Naming practice

- [Lexicon: brand naming process](https://www.lexiconbranding.com/brand-naming-process/) discusses small creative teams, linguistic work and research. It publishes an average engagement fee of US$75,000–$150,000; quoted in the README/article with a checked date, not as a market-wide average.
- [Catchword: creating a brand name](https://catchwordbranding.com/catchthis/create-the-perfect-brand-name/) is background for the brief and iterative development process.
- [Igor naming guide](https://www.igorinternational.com/guide/) is background for name approaches and comparative creative evaluation. Its original categories aren't a statistical validation of this tool's scores.
- [Zinzin's process](https://www.zinzin.com/process/) is background for competitive name patterns and positioning.

The separate generation briefs are a diversity technique inspired by professional practice. Model/context isolation must be reported accurately; it isn't a guarantee of better names. A machine pronunciation heuristic isn't a substitute for target-market feedback.

## Domain and handle evidence

- [IANA RDAP bootstrap registry](https://data.iana.org/rdap/dns.json) maps suffixes to registry services.
- [RDAP HTTP usage, RFC 7480](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7480) describes transport behaviour; a generic error from an intermediary doesn't establish a domain's availability.
- [RDAP JSON responses, RFC 9083](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9083) describes structured responses.
- [Python project-name normalisation](https://packaging.python.org/en/latest/specifications/name-normalization/) explains why PyPI punctuation variants share a namespace.

Registry/provider endpoints and policies change. The script's current observations and fixtures are the evidence for a run. Historical probes, anecdotes and market prices have deliberately not been turned into promises about current availability or competitors' tools. No-record observations still leave actual provider registration/claiming unconfirmed.
