# Sound as creative material

Sound symbolism can suggest creative directions; it does not determine a brand's success or meaning across languages. Associations depend on language, context and the audience. Do not present a letter as universally trustworthy, feminine, innovative or fast.

Try contrasting vowel shapes, stop consonants, fricatives and softer consonants to create variety. Read candidates in a carrier sentence and compare them with the desired tone. A sound-led candidate can later be associated with a genuine root; don't invent its history.

Use target-market readings to notice ambiguity, not to demand identical accents. Shorter names aren't automatically better. Test the practical job: can people recognise, say, remember and find it?

The studies and professional methods credited in `09-sources.md` are background reading. Claims about a particular candidate require its own evidence and human feedback.
